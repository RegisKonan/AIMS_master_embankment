## Title: Application of Darcy's law in a 2D porous embankment.

### Abstract: 

The increasing demand for freshwater resources and the rapid pace of environmental degradation have raised major concerns regarding the sustainable management of water systems. This thesis, entitled \textit{Application of Darcy’s Law in a 2D Porous Embankment}, aims to investigate the behavior of water flow through a porous embankment using Darcy’s law, which is the fundamental equation governing the motion of fluids in porous media. The overarching goal of this study is to determine the quantity of water that can pass through an embankment to ensure both effective conservation and the prevention of flooding risks.

The research begins with a comprehensive review of the theoretical and experimental foundations of Darcy’s law, followed by an introduction to the fundamental physical properties of porous media, such as porosity, permeability, saturation, and water content, and their influence on flow dynamics. The study also examines the concept of the representative elementary volume (REV), which allows the macroscopic characterization of porous structures, and explores both isotropic and anisotropic permeability conditions. These theoretical developments serve as a foundation for analyzing embankments as hydraulic structures, with particular emphasis on earthfill, rockfill, and reinforced embankments.

The methodology combines analytical derivation and practical application. Starting from the general form of Darcy’s law:
\[
\mathbf{U} = -\frac{K}{\mu}(\nabla p - \rho \mathbf{g}),
\]
the study derives expressions for the flow rate in both horizontal and non-horizontal porous embankments. For a horizontal embankment separating two water tables at different levels $y_0$ and $y_1$, the steady-state flow rate is expressed as:
\[
Q = \frac{K g (y_0^2 - y_1^2)}{2 \nu L},
\]
where $K$ is the permeability, $g$ the gravitational acceleration, $L$ the embankment length, and $\nu$ the kinematic viscosity of the fluid. The analysis is extended to multilayer embankments composed of materials with different permeabilities and to inclined embankments characterized by an angle $\theta$ with respect to the horizontal, leading to a generalized flow expression that accounts for gravitational components along the slope.\\

An application to a realistic earthfill embankment resting on impermeable bedrock was conducted to quantify the water flow rate through the structure. Using the parameters $y_0 = 26~\mathrm{m}$, $y_1 = 22~\mathrm{m}$, $y_2 = 18~\mathrm{m}$, $K_1 = 34 \times 10^{-12}~\mathrm{m^2}$, $K_2 = 60 \times 10^{-12}~\mathrm{m^2}$, $g = 9.81~\mathrm{m/s^2}$, $L_1 = 32~\mathrm{m}$, $L_2 - L_1 = 28~\mathrm{m}$, and $\nu = 10^{-6}~\mathrm{m^2/s}$, the computed discharge was:
\[
Q = 2.68 \times 10^{-3}~\mathrm{m^2/s},
\]
which confirms the theoretical prediction and demonstrates the applicability of the derived model to real hydraulic engineering problems.\\

The major results of this work highlight the linear dependence of the flow rate on permeability and the inverse relationship with embankment length. The analysis also reveals that the inclination angle significantly affects the magnitude of the flow, increasing it with slope steepness. Furthermore, the multilayer extension of Darcy’s law shows how the combination of materials with different permeabilities alters the total discharge and water distribution within the embankment.\\

My individual contribution to this research included the formulation of the mathematical model, derivation of the analytical expressions, and validation of the theoretical predictions using numerical computation. I also implemented graphical representations with GeoGebra to visualize the flow profiles and to illustrate the dependence of the flow rate on parameters such as permeability, slope, and water level differences. Through this independent work, I gained a deeper understanding of fluid mechanics in porous systems, the coupling between physical laws and engineering applications, and the analytical rigor required for hydrological modeling.\\

In conclusion, this thesis provides a clear and generalizable framework for evaluating flow in porous embankments under various configurations. The results can serve as a reference for the design and analysis of hydraulic structures, flood control systems, and groundwater management. Future work could extend this approach to transient flow conditions or implement finite element simulations to complement the analytical findings.
